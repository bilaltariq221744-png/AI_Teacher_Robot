Minar-E-Pakistan: A Symbol of Freedom
We all celebrate Pakistan Day on 23 March because of the history written in
1940. It was due to the historic session of the All-India Muslim League under the
chairmanship of Quaid-i-Azam Mohammad Ali Jinnah that the official call to make a
separate homeland for the Muslims of the sub-continent was given. In order to
remember the importance of the day, a very elegant structure called, Minar-e-Pakistan
was constructed in Iqbal Park, Lahore. The work began in 1960 and was completed in
eightyears.
The design of Minar-e-Pakistan is a mix of Islamic, Mughal
and contemporary architecture which was the brainchild of
Murat Khan, a Russian born Pakistani architect. The base of the
historic memorial has four levels. It is raised approximately 8
meters from the ground and is constructed exactly where the
stage was set in 1940. These levels are covered in different
varieties of rough stone surfaces while the final platform is in
polished marble, signifying the struggle of the Pakistan
movement which lead to a polished gem of an independent state. The final level is a 5-
pointstarshape,encircledinacrescentandtransformsintounfoldingflowerpetals.
